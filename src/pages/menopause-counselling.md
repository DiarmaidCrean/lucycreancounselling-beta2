 “I don’t feel like myself anymore”

This is something I often hear from women who come to therapy to support them in their midlife. Midlife can be a challenging time, and it’s a natural time to reflect and question. However, for women the challenge can be magnified due to a changing and disrupted hormonal landscape. 

Women, often the ones who just get on with it, push through with a smile on their face, park their own feelings so as not to upset the apple cart, suddenly find themselves floundering. Perhaps sandwiched between caring for children as well as aging parents, finding work challenging in a way it never was before or being confronted by huge doubts about relationships. It can be a confusing time. 

“I used to be able to cope but now I can’t”

“I feel so much rage! Why am I so angry?”

“I’m so tired of supporting everyone else!”

“I’ve lost all my confidence”

These are just some of the questions that bring women to therapy in midlife. 

During perimenopause, hormonal fluctuations can influence the systems involved in brain functioning, as well as body, affecting stress regulation and emotional responses. This can make coping mechanisms that may have once worked well, now feel completely inaccessible. The masks women may have worn to get through stressful times or social situations seem lost. Every ‘role’ adopted in relationship feels tired and inauthentic. It can feel a hugely exposing time. If you are neurodiverse (diagnosed or not) you may well have an extra layer of difficulty due to the pressure hormonal changes put on cognitive function. Its really common for midlife women to say they don’t recognise themselves.

You may be craving a space to hide away and be with yourself, a need to get some space on your own. So much to try and make sense of, and you just wish you could slowdown. Busy and demanding life usually makes this impossible. 

Therapy can give you this space - a safe harbour. Women often ask “Can therapy help me with menopause” and the answer is yes it can.

Menopause is not *just* about hormones. Yes, the hormonal landscape is changing and it is important (and hugely validating!) to understand this. But this developmental stage, is a biological, social, and psychological experience and it touches on every area of a woman’s life. There are many facets at play when trying to make sense of a woman’s midlife experiences, and its important to have a therapist who understands this.

If you are a midlife woman, menopause-informed therapy can offer you the understanding of how all of these pieces fit together, as well as the tools to move forward. I am trained in EMBERS®, a menopause based CBT, offering just this.
