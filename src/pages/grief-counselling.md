Loss comes in many forms — the death of someone close, the end of a relationship, a change in health, or the loss of a sense of who you are. All of these deserve to be taken seriously and given space.

Losing someone or something precious is deeply painful, and grief is a natural response to this. It can change our understanding and beliefs about the world and the people around us. It can alter our identity, purpose and family roles. We can often feel that others don’t get it, and this can make it can feel a deeply lonely experience.

If the relationship we had with the person or situation we have lost was complicated, then this can add another layer of challenge and confusion.

We may also hold beliefs about *the way* we grieve, and this can add extra complexity. This might show up as thoughts such as “Why haven’t I cried?” “Should I be ok by now, why does everyone else seem to have moved on?”, “Am I entitled to feel this way?”. 

You may also be holding some difficult feelings such as regret, guilt, anger or relief, which can add an extra charge.

Grief is difficult and needs time. It is a deeply personal process, that can touch every area of life. Therapy can offer you support through this profoundly challenging experience.
